All-Time MLB Home Run Leaders

MLB’s All-Time Home Run Kings

Reaching the summit of Major League Baseball’s career home run leaderboard demands an extraordinary blend of single-season peak power and two-decade durability. Only 28 hitters in MLB history have joined the exclusive 500 Home Run Club, and just four—Barry Bonds (762), Hank Aaron (755), Babe Ruth (714), and Albert Pujols (703)—have ever eclipsed 700 career homers. Notably, ballpark environment plays a fascinating role even at the top: Barry Bonds hit the majority of his late-career homers in sea-level Oracle Park (13 ft elevation), where cool marine air suppresses carry, yet still set both the single-season (73 in 2001) and all-time career records.

Peak Power Pace vs. Career Longevity

While total home runs reward longevity, comparing games played against home runs per 162 games reveals two distinct paths to baseball immortality. Pure power-rate outliers like Mark McGwire (50.4 HR per 162 games; 10.6 AB/HR) and Babe Ruth (46.2 HR per 162 games; 11.8 AB/HR) homered at historic frequencies, whereas ironmen like Hank Aaron (3,298 games), Albert Pujols (3,080 games), and Eddie Murray (3,026 games) sustained elite production over more than 20 seasons.
