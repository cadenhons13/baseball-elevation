MLB Stadiums: Elevation & Home Runs

Stadium Elevation & Home Runs Across All 30 MLB Ballparks

How many home runs are actually hit at each Major League Baseball stadium—and how do ballpark elevation and outfield dimensions interact to shape those totals? Using official MLB Statcast ball-tracking data across the Statcast Era (2015–2026) alongside single-season 2026 home run counts, we can compare all 30 current MLB ballparks by altitude, total home runs hit, home runs per game, and average home run distance:

  • Most Total Home Runs (2015–2026): Yankee Stadium (2,619 HRs; 55 ft elevation), Great American Ball Park (2,539 HRs; 482 ft), Oriole Park at Camden Yards (2,522 HRs; 20 ft), and Coors Field (2,436 HRs; 5,180 ft) top all active MLB venues.
  • Why Elevation Still Rules Ball Flight: Even though Colorado pushed Coors Field’s outfield fences far back to contain scoring, thin mile-high air propelled Coors Field to 2,436 home runs (#4 in MLB) and an MLB-best 419-foot average home run distance—nearly 30 feet longer than short-porch parks like Yankee Stadium (391 ft) and Daikin Park (386 ft). Meanwhile, sea-level Oracle Park (13 ft) sits lowest among long-tenured parks with just 1,614 home runs (1.75 HR/game).
  • Note on newer MLB venues: Truist Park (opened 2017; 1,842 HRs in 759 games), Globe Life Field (opened 2020; 1,229 HRs in 516 games), and Sutter Health Park (MLB home of the Athletics in 2025–2026; 468 HRs in 156 games, or an MLB-high 3.00 HR/game) have fewer cumulative Statcast-era games, so both total HRs and HRs per game (plus 2026 single-season HRs) are shown below.
